TEUM | between Breath and Rest Vol. 01 · Sight KOEN

Have you seen
green today?

Season 1 · Summer Sense Diving No.01

Season 1 Summer Sense Diving · This teum — Sight

My eyes worked all day,
and never once rested.

It happened in an elevator. A ride of a few seconds, and there I was, already reaching for my phone. Monitor to phone, phone back to monitor. My eyes had been working all day without stopping. And yet nothing in them felt rested.

So lately I ask myself one question. Have I seen green today? It is not a grand question. Only this: did my eyes rest on something green, even for a moment?

A color that does not try

Green is a strange color. It is not dazzling, yet it draws you. It is in no hurry, yet your eyes keep returning to it. A tree does not try to look better today than yesterday. It simply stands where it stood. And somehow that indifference becomes rest.

Maybe we have spent a long time proving things. At work, in our relationships, even in the way we rest. So when we stand before something that proves nothing at all, something in us lets go.

You don't have to go far

We travel far to recover. I have done it too. But you notice something when you come home. Recovery does not arrive at the end of a great decision. It seeps in quietly, through the gaps of an ordinary day.

The things we passed by because we were busy have not disappeared. They are exactly where they were. The flower bed outside the office. The trees along the stream. One potted plant in a neighbor's window. Summer is when all of that green is at its deepest. Which makes now the best time to ask.

— oLiHANNA

This teum's pebble · Sight

Rest your eyes on green for five minutes

Pick one green thing today, anywhere, and let your eyes stay on it for five minutes. A street tree on your way to work. A single tree outside your window. Either is fine. Don't photograph it. Don't write anything down. Just look. That is all for today.

For those five minutes, only look. Afterwards, if you suddenly feel like telling someone, reply to this letter. The green you stopped for could appear in the next one, under ‘A reader's sense’, anonymously.

Have you seen green today?
That is enough.
