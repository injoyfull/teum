Have you ever
floated on water?

Season 1 · Summer Sense Diving No.06

Season 1 Summer Sense Diving · This teum — The sense with no name

I thought letting go would sink me.
Letting go is what floats you.

“Relax.” It's the thing you hear most when you're learning to swim. Which is strange, because nobody has ever relaxed on being told to relax. You try, and try, and your shoulders climb higher.

And then at some moment, entirely by accident, one moment. The water holds you. It's as if the space under your back widens, and your weight goes somewhere else. When your ears go under, the world moves a hand's width away. What are we supposed to call that unfamiliar lightness? The dictionary says buoyancy, but that word doesn't have the feeling in it.

Try to do it well and you sink

Floating requires no qualifications. Trying harder doesn't make the water hold you more. If anything, the opposite. Hand yourself over, and the water carries you.

I think I lived in the trying posture for quite a long time. Shoulders tight, chin tucked, determined not to sink. Not in water — in ordinary days. Maybe that's why the instruction was so hard. I had hardly ever let go.

It was only covered for a while

This summer I sent six letters. We looked at green, put our hands in water, listened to rain to the end, took a first bite with our eyes closed, stood where shade met sun, and today, floated. Come to think of it, all of it was practice in letting go. And it wasn't something I did alone. There were people on the other side of these letters, closing their eyes with me, putting their hands in.

One thing I learned along the way. A sense going dull doesn't mean it's broken. It was only covered for a while. Busy days piled on top of it. And what is covered can be uncovered. I lifted it, and the green was right where it had been. The textures too, the sounds, the tastes, the temperatures. You can come back. Any time at all.

— oLiHANNA

This teum's pebble · The sense with no name

Tonight, hand over all of your weight

If you have a bathtub, lie in the water until your ears go under. If not, your bed will do. Once you're lying down, take five minutes to find where the effort is still held. Shoulders, jaw, hands. When you find it, quietly let that weight go. Try thinking of it not as you lying down, but as the bed holding you up.

Where was the effort still being held? Just reply to this letter. The weight you let go of could appear in the first letter of the next season, under ‘A reader's sense’, anonymously.

Have you ever floated on water?
It was all right to let go.
