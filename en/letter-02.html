When did you last put
your hand in water?

Season 1 · Summer Sense Diving No.02

Season 1 Summer Sense Diving · This teum — Touch

I touched so many things,
and felt almost none of them.

I stopped in the middle of the dishes. The water was running. And I couldn't have told you whether it was cold or lukewarm. My hand was right there in it.

A keyboard. A steering wheel. A doorknob. A child's back. Count what your hands touch in a day and it comes to a lot. And yet almost none of it is remembered as touch. My hands were always busy doing something. Rarely were they used to feel something.

Water only speaks to hands

One of the good things about summer is how much more there is to touch. Stream water, tap water, the outside of a glass with ice in it, the back of a hand caught in a shower. You can't tell much about water by looking. You have to put your hand in. Whether it's cold. Whether it's slippery. How heavy it feels.

So water is a little unusual. It says nothing at all unless you touch it. Some things can never be known from a distance. Water is exactly like that.

Children put their hands in first

Spend time around children and you notice something. When there's water, children don't think. Their hands go in first. Splash. The adults ask from the side, “Is it cold?” But the child already knows. They've already touched it.

When did it start? We began thinking before touching. Will it be cold. It's a nuisance if I get wet. Do I even have time for this. And while we're weighing it, the summer goes by.

— oLiHANNA

This teum's pebble · Touch

Put your hand in water for five minutes

In the middle of the dishes is fine. Washing your hands is fine. Just once, stop what you're doing and let your hand rest in the water alone. Cold or lukewarm. Which knows first, the back of your hand or the palm. You could even skip the towel and wait for it to dry. Touching in order to feel — it's probably been a while.
