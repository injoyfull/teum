Do you remember the first
bite of watermelon?

Season 1 · Summer Sense Diving No.04

Season 1 Summer Sense Diving · This teum — Taste & Smell

I ate the whole thing,
and the taste was only in the first bite.

I cut a watermelon, set it on the table, and picked up a slice. First bite. Cool and sweet. And from the second slice on… I don't really remember. I was looking at my phone.

Isn't that strange. I must have eaten ten slices, and the taste lives in one bite. Where did the other nine go?

It was never about the fruit

I once drew summer fruit with a group of older people. “Summer fruit — that'd be watermelon, and Korean melon.” The words came easily. Their hands didn't move so easily. They hesitated a long while, and then the stories came out. Not stories about fruit, but about the summers they ate that fruit in. And while they talked, their faces opened up.

Taste never arrives alone. It always brings something with it. The wooden floor of that day, the people, the heat of it. So when you lose the taste, you lose that summer along with it.

Smell lasts longer

A watermelon smells from the moment you cut it. That green, cool smell that spreads through the kitchen the second the knife goes in. Taste waits until it's in your mouth. Smell is already there before that. And it stays longer.

I love the smell of damp earth after rain stops. There's someone that smell brings back to me. Faster than a photograph. More accurate than a name.

Smell can't be explained. You simply know. Perhaps because it's the oldest sense we have.

— oLiHANNA

This teum's pebble · Taste & Smell

Take the first bite with your eyes closed

Whatever you eat today, take only the first bite with your eyes closed. Turn your phone face down. Just that one bite. With your eyes closed the smell arrives first. The taste comes after. Whether it's cold, when the sweetness comes, what stays after you swallow. That one bite will last longer than ten slices.

What was your first bite today? With your eyes closed, what arrived first? Just reply to this letter. That one bite could appear in the next one, under ‘A reader's sense’, anonymously.
